How encounters begin

A woman opens the conversation. An invitation gives the day a new direction.

Women make the first move

Sire responds to the connection they begin.

Sire never initiates the first personal encounter. A woman, or women, approach him. That is a defining part of how his stories begin.

He might be finishing a reading, exploring a city, helping with a project or simply having a meal. Someone has a reason to speak to him. She may know his writing, be curious about his work or have an offer that changes what he thought he was doing next.

An offer has a person behind it

A singer starts a conversation after her performance. An envoy opens a door into a hidden civilisation. A reader asks him a question about a passage that stayed with her. A group of women invite him to join something they are creating.

These are possibilities for scenes. Each woman brings a voice, an intention and a life of her own. Sire gets to listen, ask questions and discover what the invitation might mean for them together.

Serendipity has consequences

The universe unfolds through these meetings. An invitation can change an afternoon or open a much longer relationship. It can take him into a new place, a shared project or a way of living he had only explored on the page.

He is receptive and involved. He can be surprised, amused, attracted, uncertain or delighted. The story develops through what they choose to share and explore.

She has something in mind. He has time to listen.

His boldness lives in what he is willing to imagine and participate in. The pleasure is in an encounter unfolding naturally, with mutual interest and the freedom to discover where it goes.
