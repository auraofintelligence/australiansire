Global Group Marriages

Love, desire and shared commitments across countries. Sire’s life keeps moving.

A main exploration of the series

A travelling writer, ongoing relationships and a world of places to meet again.

Sire is not the settling-down kind. Global Group Marriages is one of the main ways the series explores love, desire and lasting connection within a life that keeps moving.

He wants to experience every country and territory. Along the way, women initiate encounters that may become a shared adventure, an ongoing relationship or an invitation into a larger marriage. The possibilities grow through those meetings.

Several people, several places

The wider Global Group Marriages proposal begins with three or more adults choosing to understand their relationships as a larger marriage. The bonds within it can be romantic, sexual, companionate, creative or centred on parenting. Each connection contributes something different to their shared life.

The marriage can span countries and connected homes. Some people may travel together; others may meet again in a place they know or somewhere new. Each relationship has its own history, attraction and rhythm, while the wider group develops things they share.

For Sire, a visit can deepen a relationship without becoming the end of his travels. Returning can be as exciting as arriving for the first time. There is someone he wants to see, a conversation to continue, a celebration to join or something they have been looking forward to doing together.

More people, more possibilities

Global Group Marriages explores what several people can make possible together: affection, intimacy, practical support, shared income, places to stay and a wider mix of skills. Someone’s pleasure in travelling can sit alongside another person’s love of teaching, making music, growing food or creating a welcoming place.

Sire brings writing, curiosity, experience and the connections he makes along the way. The people he meets bring their own capabilities and ambitions. The series can explore how that shared capacity gives them more time, freedom and opportunities to enjoy life together.

Desire and future generations

The exploration includes sexual attraction, fertility, pregnancy and the possibility of making and raising children together. These are substantial themes in his imagined future. They sit alongside affection, humour, cultural exchange and the pleasure of being wanted.

That gives the fiction personal questions to explore: how do they share time across distance, what do they want from one another, and what does being involved look like as their lives change? Where children become part of a story, their care and relationships continue through the journeys too.

The world keeps opening

The travel oracle can bring a gathering, a reader’s invitation, a creative opportunity and time with an existing partner into the same journey. The Forge connects Global Group Marriages with local groups and meetups, making relationships part of how a worthwhile next destination comes into view.

The U.N. of Love

The U.N. of Love takes the idea into a particular thought experiment: adults from many cultures sharing a loving, sexually alive marriage and raising children together. Languages, food, stories, celebrations and ways of thinking become part of an intimate shared life.

Its questions reach into diplomacy and collective intelligence. How do they make decisions together, share resources, resolve a disagreement and learn from one another? Peace becomes something practised between people who know and love each other. Future generations are their own children, with lives and cultures growing around them.

This gives Sire’s books a way to connect the personal with the planetary. The Forge takes another leap with a family spread across Earth, orbit and off-world places, where relationships continue across light-minutes. The imagined future can be expansive while the story stays close to the people living it.

Stories that continue between visits

A group of readers might invite him to a gathering after recognising something of their own hopes in his books. Partners might travel with him for part of a journey. A return visit might bring a shared celebration or a conversation that changes their plans. These are possible story directions, with the people and their choices shaping what follows.

Sire keeps writing as that life unfolds. Global Group Marriages gives the series an ongoing thread: how far can a shared life reach while he continues exploring?

Explore the idea further

The Global Group Marriages framework develops shared love, care, resources and choice. Its relationship models include connected homes and marriages across countries. The U.N. of Love and GAJRA Earth extend those ideas into cross-cultural and planetary life.

The World Law Atlas examines present-day recognition, parentage, property, movement and care country by country. Sire’s fiction explores imagined lives through the Story Forge.
